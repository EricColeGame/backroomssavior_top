If you have ever lost an evening to grainy footage of endless yellow hallways, you already know why this topic matters. Backrooms explained in plain language is the goal here: what the Backrooms actually are, where the idea came from, and how the many versions of it fit together. The short answer is that the Backrooms are a collaborative internet horror setting — a dimension of infinite, badly lit liminal spaces you reach by "noclipping" out of reality. The longer answer involves a 2019 forum post, sprawling fan wikis, a viral found-footage series, indie games, and a Hollywood adaptation in development. Getting backrooms explained properly matters because the setting has no single canon, and that is exactly what trips newcomers up.

## What Are the Backrooms? The Core Idea in Plain Terms

The Backrooms are a fictional dimension that sits "behind" everyday reality. You do not find a door into it. You slip through a crack in the world — a process borrowed from video game vocabulary and called **noclipping**. The founding premise describes the smell of old, damp carpet, the "madness of mono-yellow," the endless hum of fluorescent lights at maximum buzz, and roughly 600 million square miles of randomly segmented empty rooms.

What makes it frightening is not gore. It is **liminality**: spaces that feel familiar but wrong. An empty office corridor at 3 a.m. A drained swimming pool. A hotel hallway with no guests. The Backrooms take that uneasy feeling and stretch it to infinity, then add the possibility that something nearby has already heard you.

| Term | What it means |
|---|---|
| Noclipping | Slipping out of reality into the Backrooms, borrowed from video game clipping bugs |
| Level | A distinct region of the Backrooms with its own layout, hazards, and exits |
| Entity | Any non-human creature inhabiting a level |
| Almond Water | A widely referenced item said to calm the mind and restore sanity |
| M.E.G. | Major Explorer Group, the largest documented explorer faction in fan lore |
| Outpost | A fortified safe zone maintained by a faction on a specific level |
| Liminal space | A transitional place that feels empty, familiar, and subtly unsettling |

## The Origins: From One Forum Post to a Global Franchise

The concept began as a single short piece of horror writing posted to the 4chan /x/ board in 2019, paired with a photograph of a yellow, carpeted room. That image did most of the heavy lifting. Community researchers later traced the photo to a hobby shop in Oshkosh, Wisconsin, taken during a renovation in the early 2000s — which is a neat reminder that the most unsettling images are usually just ordinary rooms photographed badly.

From there, the setting escaped its source. Fan wikis turned one paragraph into an entire cosmology, complete with numbered levels, factions, and survival manuals. Then came video.

| Year | Milestone |
|---|---|
| 2019 | The original post introduces the premise and the yellow-room image |
| 2019–2021 | Fan wikis expand it into hundreds of numbered levels and entity profiles |
| 2022 | Kane Parsons ("Kane Pixels") releases *The Backrooms (Found Footage)*, which goes viral |
| 2022 | Independent found-footage features and the first wave of Backrooms games appear |
| 2023 | A24 announces a feature adaptation with Parsons attached to direct |
| Present day | Co-op horror games, Roblox experiences, and short films keep the setting alive |

Parsons' series is the single most important adaptation for newcomers. It reframes the Backrooms as the result of a research accident and gives the setting something the original post never had: a plot, a timeline, and a company that knows more than it admits.

## Backrooms Levels Explained: The Floors You'll Hear About Most

Here is the part where backrooms explained gets messy. Levels are **not** a staircase you descend. They are separate regions connected by specific exits — a door, a hole in a wall, a wrong turn, or another noclip. Two explorers can enter the same level and end up in completely different places.

Level numbering also differs between wikis, which is why two fans can argue for an hour and both be right within their own canon. Treat the list below as the community's shared shorthand rather than a fixed map.

| Level | Nickname | What to expect |
|---|---|---|
| Level 0 | The Lobby | Mono-yellow rooms, damp carpet, buzzing lights; the classic starting point |
| Level 1 | Habitable Zone | Concrete floors, warehouse feel, M.E.G. outposts, Hound sightings |
| Level 2 | Pipe Dreams | Narrow industrial corridors, extreme heat, poor visibility |
| Level 5 | Terror Hotel | A grand, decaying hotel with a persistent sense of being watched |
| Level 6 | Lights Out | Total darkness; navigation depends entirely on sound and memory |
| Level 9 | The Suburbs | Endless night-time suburbia with houses that never end |
| Level 37 | Sublimity (Poolrooms) | Bright tiled pools and warm water — beautiful, disorienting, often quiet |
| Level ! | Run For Your Life | A corridor of entities; community reports describe it as pure flight |

The Poolrooms deserve a special mention. They are the aesthetic opposite of Level 0 — bright, calm, and almost pleasant — yet they produce the same creeping dread. That contrast is a big part of why the setting keeps drawing in new fans.

## Entities, Items, and Groups: Backrooms Explained for Survivors

Almost everything that hunts you in the Backrooms was invented by fans, not by the original post. That means the roster changes depending on which wiki you read. The entries below are the ones that appear most consistently across community reports.

| Entity | Typical habitat | Threat level | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Smilers | Dark levels | High | A grin of glowing teeth in total darkness |
| Hounds | Level 1, Level 2, industrial levels | High | Fast, territorial, and rarely alone |
| Partygoers | Level Fun and its offshoots | Extreme | Cheerful entities that convert explorers into their own kind |
| Skin-Stealers | Wandering levels | Extreme | Mimic human beings; the reason not to trust strangers |
| Facelings | Level 9, Level 11, hubs | Variable | Featureless humanoids; often neutral, sometimes hostile |
| Deathmoths | Lit corridors and Level 5 | Moderate | Large moth-like creatures drawn to flashlights |
| Wretches | Level 2 and similar | Moderate | Blind hunters that track movement and sound |

Survival advice in the wikis tends to converge on a few rules, and they are worth internalizing before you play any Backrooms game:

- **Carry Almond Water.** It is the setting's universal "reset button" for panic and disorientation.
- **Light is a trade-off.** A flashlight helps you see and simultaneously announces your position.
- **Do not follow friendly voices.** Mimicry is the most common trap in the lore.
- **Mark your route.** Chalk, tape, or notes on walls; backtracking is how most people get home.
- **Stay out of Level Fun.** No exception, no curiosity.

Beyond creatures, the setting has politics. The **M.E.G.** is the closest thing to a government, running outposts and publishing survival guides. The **Backrooms Colonists** represent a rival philosophy, the **Party Crashers** exist specifically to fight Partygoers, and a large population of unaffiliated wanderers simply tries to survive. Player experience in co-op games reflects this well: groups that agree on rules early tend to last far longer than groups that improvise.

## Which Version Is Canon? Backrooms Explained Across Wikis, Games, and Film

There is no official Backrooms. There is a founding text, a pile of fan wikis, and a growing list of adaptations that each treat the premise differently. Understanding that structure is the fastest way to stop being confused by contradictions.

| Source | Scope | Canon weight |
|---|---|---|
| Original 2019 forum post | The premise and one photograph | Founding text |
| [Backrooms Wiki on Wikidot](https://backrooms-wiki.wikidot.com/) | The most detailed level, entity, and faction database | Dominant fan canon |
| Fandom Backrooms Wiki | Alternative level numbering and entity lore | Parallel fan canon |
| Kane Parsons' found-footage series | A research-accident origin story with a plot | Popular cinematic canon |
| [A24](https://a24films.com/) feature adaptation | Feature-length film in development | Future film canon |
| Steam, itch.io, and Roblox games | Gameplay-first interpretations with puzzles and co-op | Game canon |

Because these canons overlap without agreeing, the healthiest approach is to pick one as your home base and treat the rest as alternate timelines. Most fans start with the Wikidot wiki for lore depth and with Parsons' videos for atmosphere.

## FAQ

**Are the Backrooms real?**
No. The Backrooms are a collaborative work of internet horror fiction. The original photograph is a real room in a real building, but the dimension, levels, and entities are invented. That said, the liminal-space feeling they imitate is real, which is precisely why the concept works.

**What is Level 0, in short?**
Level 0, also called The Lobby, is the classic starting point: yellow wallpaper, damp carpet, buzzing fluorescent lights, and an endless maze of empty rooms. Most backrooms explained breakdowns begin here because it defines the visual language of the entire setting.

**Do I need to read the wikis in order?**
No. Levels are connected by exits rather than sequence, and each wiki maintains its own numbering. Pick a level that sounds interesting and read outward from there.

**Why do fans disagree about the lore?**
Because there is no single author. Thousands of contributors have added levels, entities, and factions over several years, and adaptations like the found-footage series and the A24 film introduce their own versions. Treat it like folklore: shared themes, competing tellings, and no final word.
