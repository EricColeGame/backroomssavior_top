You survived the yellow halls — now the concrete begins. **Backrooms Level 1**, better known as the "Habitable Zone," is the second level most wanderers reach, and it trades damp carpet and buzzing fluorescent lights for cold parking-garage corridors, wooden crates, and an entirely new set of threats. This **Backrooms Level 1** guide covers what a newcomer actually needs: how the level's two zones work, how to navigate non-Euclidean halls without losing your mind, what to pull out of crates first, and how to survive a Flickering event.

Most of what follows is drawn from community-maintained documentation, so treat it as player experience rather than gospel. The Backrooms have no official manual — only the notes other wanderers leave behind. If you want the raw source material, the [Backrooms Wiki's Level 1 entry](https://backrooms-wiki.wikidot.com/level-1) is the reference most groups cite.

## What Is Backrooms Level 1? The Habitable Zone Explained

Backrooms Level 1 is the level you reach after no-clipping out of Level 0. The relief is real: the endless hum is gone, the air is drier, and the floor is concrete instead of rotting carpet. What replaces it, though, is scale. Community reports describe the level as a chimera between an underground parking structure and an abandoned warehouse — grey walls, concrete pillars, leaking ceiling pipes, and puddles you should never drink from.

Its survival difficulty is rated low, which is misleading. The level is *habitable*, not harmless. It has resources, settlements, and friendly patrols — and it also has entities, unpredictable blackouts, and humans who will rob you as quickly as any monster.

| Feature | Level 0 (The Yellow Halls) | Backrooms Level 1 (Habitable Zone) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Environment | Mono-yellow rooms, damp carpet | Grey concrete halls, pillars, warehouse shelving |
| Sound | Constant hum and buzz | Mostly silent, occasional pipe clangs |
| Resources | Almost none | Crates with food, water, and tools |
| Threat level | Low, but deeply isolating | Moderate — entities, Flickering, hostile humans |
| Human presence | Rare | Common — bases, outposts, patrols |
| Navigation | Endless but simple | Non-Euclidean and deceptive |

## Halls, Corridors, and Sectors: How Backrooms Level 1 Is Laid Out

The level splits into two distinct zones. Understanding the difference is the single most useful thing you can learn early.

**The halls** are the wide, open areas you arrive in: bland discolored walls, concrete pillars holding up a structure that seems to stretch for miles, and shelves or floors where crates materialize. The halls are where resources are, and where danger lives.

**The corridors** sit behind single or double push doors, usually marked with a green exit sign. They are tight, dim, and labyrinthine — but crucially, their geometry behaves normally. Your senses work again in the corridors, which makes them the safest place to sleep.

| Trait | Halls | Corridors |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Layout | Wide, open, non-Euclidean | Tight, maze-like, normal geometry |
| Navigation | Requires landmarks | Trust your eyes |
| Resources | Crates appear here | Scarce — stock up before entering |
| Danger | Entities, especially during Flickering | Rarely patrolled by entities |
| Best use | Travel and scavenging | Rest, camps, long-term shelter |
| Rooms | Open floor and shelving | Small purposeless rooms behind doors |

Wanderers also divide the level into **sectors** — sub-regions with slightly different pillar shapes and wall colors that help you figure out roughly where you are. Only a few are well documented.

| Sector | Community notes |
| --- | --- |
| Aquila Sector | The best-documented area: grey parking-lot architecture, leaking ceiling pipes, and M.E.G. Base Alpha. Most newcomers no-clip in here. |
| Gild Sector | Referenced as the home of Trader's Keep operations and trade activity. |
| Gothic Sector | Reportedly distinguished by a darker, more worn palette and different pillar forms. |
| Ouroboros Sector | Used mainly as a positional marker by exploring groups. |
| Garden Sector | Subtle architectural variation; documentation is sparse. |
| Fabled Sector | Rarely documented and treated as a distant reference zone. |

## Navigating Backrooms Level 1 Without Getting Lost

The halls obey what the community calls non-Euclidean geometry. Distances you perceive are fabricated. You can walk for thirty seconds and cross what looked like a horizon, or wander for ten minutes and realize you barely moved. This is the number one cause of death among newcomers — not because the geometry kills you, but because it strands you in the open when the lights go out.

| Step | Action | Why it works |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Pick a fixed landmark — a colored pillar, table, electrical panel, or crate | Gives you a reference point your eyes can't fake |
| 2 | Walk toward it without looking away | Prevents drift caused by shifting apparent distances |
| 3 | If you aren't getting closer, switch landmarks | The object is much farther away than it appears |
| 4 | Repeat until you reach a corridor door | Corridors use normal geometry, so navigation gets easy |

Experienced wanderers eventually stop needing landmarks, but the wiki warns against that confidence. Overestimating your sense of direction is a common way to die on Backrooms Level 1.

## Crates, Almond Water, and Gear Priority on Backrooms Level 1

Crates are the reason this level is called habitable. These featureless wooden boxes appear on shelves, on the floor, or seemingly behind your back after a blackout — and they can vanish once you stop looking at them. Take everything you need before you walk away.

The signature resource is **Almond Water**, a bottled drink that restores hydration and, according to countless player reports, clears headaches and sharpens focus. Beyond that, crates hold a genuinely random assortment of items, including things that will cut you.

| Priority | Items to grab | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Immediate | Almond Water, canned or jarred food | Dehydration and hunger are the fastest threats |
| Early | Clean warm clothes, spare outfit, sturdy footwear | You will walk for hours looking for people |
| Medical | Bandages, gauze, antiseptic, soap | Crate cuts and scrapes infect easily |
| Tools | Knife or axe, matches or lighter, flashlight | Opening crates, warmth, visibility in corridors |
| Comfort | Yarn, notepad and pen, watch, sleeping bag | Morale and timekeeping keep you functional |

Two rules from the collectors' guides are worth repeating. First, never shove your hand into a crate blind — glass shards, chemicals, and worse have all been found inside. Second, don't over-encumber yourself. If you can't run, you can't escape an entity, and a backpack full of "maybe useful" junk is how that happens.

## Flickering, Entities, and Safe Settlements on Backrooms Level 1

**Flickering** is the level's defining hazard. Without warning, every light in the halls cuts out and the level goes pitch black. The darkness itself isn't the killer — the entities that use it to hunt are. Events can start and end at any time, so you cannot plan around them.

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| Move toward the nearest corridor door, marked by a faint green exit light | Turn on a flashlight or phone light in the halls — you become the only target |
| Follow the wall with your hand to stay oriented | Sprint — entities hear even faint sounds |
| Stay quiet and barricade yourself in a room | Rely on a natural door lock; they can disappear when looked at |
| Wait for the lights to return before moving on | Assume the blackout will be short |

Entities on Backrooms Level 1 range from neutral to predatory. Community documentation describes humanoid creatures — including ones that mimic distressed humans and shed a victim's skin — as some of the most dangerous encounters. The general advice is consistent: don't run in the open halls, don't fire a weapon at range, don't pick a close fight, and never approach a lone humanoid figure. Real wanderers usually travel in groups.

Settlements are your long-term survival plan. Groups mark supply caches with brightly colored cloth, a logo, and a note explaining how to reach their outpost — take the supplies, leave the cloth on the floor so patrols know to restock. Major presences include M.E.G. (with Base Alpha in the Aquila Sector), the B.N.T.G., the Ariane Circle, Camp Amber, Eyes of Argos, Speednoclippers, and Tom's Diner. Not every settlement is friendly.

| Direction | Route | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Enter | Level 0 — The Yellow Halls | The most common arrival, via no-clipping |
| Enter | Level 4.2 — Parking Lot of Fog | Certain random doors |
| Enter | Level 11 — The Endless City | Some building entrances |
| Exit | Level 2 — Abandoned Utility Halls | A smooth threshold: pipes and dials gradually appear |
| Exit | Level 22 — Ruins Left Behind | Glass doors |
| Exit | Level 154 — The Laser Game | Doors with unique symbols at the end of lit corridors |

If you no-clip somewhere unfamiliar, community groups recommend the C.L.I.P.P. protocol: check your environment, locate an exit, inspect your database, prepare your equipment, and perimeter a temporary safe zone.

## FAQ

**Is Backrooms Level 1 safe?**
It's rated as one of the safer levels, but "safe" is relative. The halls are dangerous during Flickering events, crates can contain hazards, and other humans can be as threatening as entities. The corridors are the genuinely safe zone.

**How do you reach Backrooms Level 1?**
Most wanderers arrive by no-clipping out of Level 0, the Yellow Halls. Other documented entrances include random doors on Level 4.2 and certain building entrances on Level 11.

**What should you do during a Flickering event on Backrooms Level 1?**
Find the nearest corridor door by looking for its green exit light, keep one hand on the wall to stay oriented, stay quiet, and never use a light source in the open halls. Once inside a corridor room, barricade the door and wait for the lights.

**Can you find other people on Backrooms Level 1?**
Yes — it's one of the most populated levels in the Backrooms. Look for colored cloth markers with group logos and notes, or wait for an M.E.G. patrol in the Aquila Sector. Approach cautiously, and remember that not every group is friendly.
